📻 802.11 — Wi-Fi standard

Defines the MAC + PHY, the frame types, the access method (CSMA/CA), and the amendment generations (a/b/g/n/ac/ax).

Frame types

Management

beacon, probe, auth, assoc

Control

CSMA/CA — why wireless can't detect collisions

1 Sense the channel
2 Random backoff (DIFS/AIFS)
3 Send if clear
4 Receiver ACKs
Wireless can't detect collisions → CSMA/CA with ACKs (no collision detection like wired CSMA/CD).

🏷️ SSID & BSSID

Two distinct identities: the SSID is the human name; the BSSID is the AP radio's MAC.

SSID vs BSSID (the core trap)

TermTypeMeaning
SSIDASCII name (≤32 chars)network identity
BSSID48-bit MACspecific AP radio
BSSone cell1 AP radio + clients
ESSmany BSSesone SSID across APs
One SSID can span many BSSIDs (one per AP radio/band). Same SSID, different BSSIDs is normal in an ESS.

The identity split

SSID

name clients see

BSSID

AP radio MAC

BSS

one AP cell

ESS

all cells, one SSID

SSID broadcast ≠ secure — hidden SSIDs are still probed by clients. BSSID = beacon source / frame destination.

🔒 CAPWAP — AP ⇄ WLC tunnel

The standard tunnel between AP and WLC, carrying control (and in Local mode) data. Two logical tunnels, two ports, DTLS-secured.

The two tunnels (memorize the ports)

TunnelPortContent
ControlUDP 5246config, monitor
DataUDP 5247client traffic
Control = UDP 5246; Data = UDP 5247. Control is DTLS-encrypted; data can use DTLS too.

Discovery → join → config

1 AP finds WLC (broadcast/multicast/DNS/hostname)
2 discovery request/response
3 join request/response
4 config push (+ software)

Mode data-path recap

ModeData over CAPWAP
Localyes (to WLC)
FlexConnectno (AP switches locally)
Monitorcontrol only
Only Local mode sends client data to the WLC over CAPWAP — making the WLC a potential chokepoint. CAPWAP replaced legacy LWAPP.

🔄 Mobility

Keeps a client's session alive while it moves between APs and even between WLCs. WLCs join a mobility group and share client state.

Mobility group

WLCs that trust each other and exchange client state (MAC/IP, security) — the foundation for seamless roaming.

Roles during a roam

Anchor

first WLC — holds the session/subnet

Foreign

Controller

group member — coordinates

Anchor = where the session lives; foreign = where the client is now.

🔐 WPA2 & WPA3 Security

Two generations. WPA2 = AES-CCMP + PSK/802.1X. WPA3 = SAE (kills offline dictionary attacks), forward secrecy, mandatory PMF.

WPA2 vs WPA3 (the headline table)

FeatureWPA2WPA3
PersonalPSK 4-way handshakeSAE
Dict attackvulnerableresistant
Forward secrecynoyes
PMFoptionalmandatory
Enterprise802.1X+ 192-bit
Offline dictionary attack is the headline WPA3 fix — SAE (Simultaneous Authentication of Equals) replaces the PSK model.

WPA2 modes & encryption

Personal

PSK (shared passphrase) — small networks

Enterprise

802.1X/RADIUS — per-user

AES-CCMP is the WPA2 cipher (IEEE 802.11i); it replaced weak TKIP/RC4 (WPA1).

4-way handshake (WPA2) → PTK

1 AP sends ANonce
2 client sends SNonce + MIC
3 AP confirms + installs key
4 client confirms
Both sides derive the PTK from ANonce/SNonce + PMK. In Enterprise, RADIUS hands the MSK which becomes the PMK.

WPA3 highlights

SAE

personal · resists offline dict

PMF mandatory

stops deauth/forgery

192-bit

enterprise high-assurance

Transition mode lets legacy WPA2 devices join but is a weaker mixed baseline — prefer WPA3-only where possible.
